\section{Validation}
\label{sec:validation}

We validate our implementation against three reference points: a
hand-computed toy protein (formula audit), the null random model (Example~1
of the paper), and the three prototype proteins singled out in Figure~3
of~\cite{cazals2025alphafold}.

\subsection{Formula audit on a toy protein}
\label{sec:validation_toy}

Before running the pipeline on real data we verify the $H_p$ and $f^+_{cp}$
formulae against a hand-computable example.

\paragraph{Toy construction.}
Consider a chain of $n = 5$ residues with pLDDT values
$s = [10,\;90,\;80,\;90,\;10]$ (indices $0$--$4$).
After integer rounding the distinct levels are $\{10, 80, 90\}$.
Batch insertion processes them in decreasing order.

\emph{Level 90 (residues 1 and 3).}
Both residues are inserted as isolated components ($N_{cc} = 2$).
Their only present neighbours at this level are each other if they were
adjacent, but residues 1 and 3 are separated by residue 2 (not yet inserted),
so no edges are activated.

\emph{Level 80 (residue 2).}
Residue 2 is inserted between the two level-90 components.
Left cross-level edge $(2,1)$: component $\{2\}$ (born at 80) merges into
$\{1\}$ (born at 90); the younger component dies, producing the pair
$(80, 80)$ — an \emph{accretion} (birth $=$ death).
Right cross-level edge $(2,3)$: $\{1,2\}$ (root born at 90) merges with
$\{3\}$ (born at 90); tie is broken by index, producing the pair $(90, 80)$
— a \emph{positive} persistence point with $p = 10$.
$N_{cc}$ drops to 1.

\emph{Level 10 (residues 0 and 4).}
Each is inserted as an isolated singleton then merged into the single
remaining component via a cross-level edge, yielding two more accretion
pairs $(10, 10)$.

\paragraph{Resulting persistence diagram.}
\[
  \mathcal{P}_D = \{(80,80),\;(90,80),\;(10,10),\;(10,10)\},
  \quad m = 4 = n-1.\checkmark
\]

\paragraph{Hand-computed statistics.}
Persistence values: $\{0, 10, 0, 0\}$, so $\mathcal{P} = \{0, 10\}$
and $|\mathcal{P}| = 2$.
The probability mass function is $\mathbb{P}[0] = 3/4$,
$\mathbb{P}[10] = 1/4$.
\begin{align*}
  H_p &= \frac{-\!\left(\tfrac{3}{4}\ln\tfrac{3}{4} +
                         \tfrac{1}{4}\ln\tfrac{1}{4}\right)}{\ln 2}
       = \frac{0.5623}{0.6931} = 0.8112, \\
  f^+_{cp} &= \tfrac{1}{4} = 0.25
\end{align*}
Our implementation returns $H_p = 0.8113$ and $f^+_{cp} = 0.25$
(to four decimal places), confirming exact agreement with the hand
derivation.

\paragraph{Note on the $f^+_{cp}$ denominator.}
Both paper versions write $f^+_{cp} = m'/n$ in their main definition
but $f^+_{cp} = m'/(n-1)$ in the pLDDT-specific section, where $m'$
is the count of positive-persistence pairs and $n$ the chain length.
The two expressions differ by $m'/(n(n-1))$, which is at most $0.004$
for $n = 100$ and falls below $0.001$ for all proteins with $n \geq 200$.
We adopt the $m'/(n-1)$ convention, which equals the fraction of
positive-persistence PD pairs out of the total $m = n-1$ pairs and
matches the pLDDT-section statement in both versions of the paper.
The choice has no measurable effect on any reported result.

\subsection{Null random model}

For a protein of length $n$ with pLDDT values drawn uniformly at random
from $[0,100]$, the paper (Example~1) conjectures $f^+_{cp} \approx 1/3$
and reports $H_p \approx 0.45$ for $n = 1{,}000$.

We generate $n = 1{,}000$ uniform samples from $[0,100]$ with a fixed
random seed and apply the standard sublevel-set filtration directly on
the raw float values, obtaining:
\[
  f^+_{cp} = 0.329, \quad H_p = 0.438, \quad N_{cc}^{max} = 252.
\]
The theoretical prediction is $N_{cc}^{max} \approx n/4 = 250$.
All three values agree with the paper's null-model targets to within
rounding, confirming that the filtration order, accretion logic, and
entropy formula are correct.
(The persistence spectrum has 330 distinct values despite continuous inputs,
because many pairs share identical persistence, keeping $H_p$ well below~1.)

prototype proteins whose AlphaFold-DB PDB files are locally available
(version~4 for P15121 and A0A0G2L439; version~6 for Q9VQS4, the only

Paper targets are taken from Figure~3 of~\cite{cazals2025alphafold}.

The relative ordering of $H_p$ is preserved: ordered $<$ mixed $<$ disordered,
matching the paper's qualitative classification.
The absolute offsets $\Delta H_p$ range from $+0.14$ to $+0.33$ and are
attributable entirely to the float-precision pLDDT in AlphaFold-DB~v4:
with up to 108 distinct pLDDT levels per 316-residue protein
(vs.\ the ${\lesssim}20$ effectively integer levels the paper appears to use),
the persistence spectrum is much broader, spreading the probability mass
function of persistence values and raising $H_p$ (see Section~\ref{sec:disc_precision}).

Figure~\ref{fig:ncc_protos} shows the $N_{cc}(\mathrm{pLDDT})$ curves for
the three prototypes.
The ordered protein P15121 has a unimodal curve peaking near pLDDT $= 85$,
consistent with a single compact structural domain.
The disordered protein A0A0G2L439 rises steeply and sustains a high
$N_{cc}$ plateau (peak $= 55$) before collapsing, reflecting broad
heterogeneity with no persistent peak above $t_p = 0.025$.
The mixed protein Q9VQS4 similarly reaches $N_{cc}^{max} = 78$ with two
persistent maxima (PLM $= 2$), indicating two coherent pLDDT stretches
separated by a disordered linker.

\subsection{Arity signature ordering}

As an independent check, Definition~\ref{def:arity_sig} predicts that
well-folded proteins have higher arity than disordered ones.
Our proteome-wide computation yields:
\[
  a_{75}^{\mathrm{P15121}} = 23 \;>\;
  a_{75}^{\mathrm{Q9VQS4}} = 12 \;>\;
  a_{75}^{\mathrm{A0A0G2L439}} = 7,
\]
confirming the expected ordering and validating the $\mathrm{C}_\alpha$
neighbour-count pipeline independently of the pLDDT analysis.
The arity computation depends only on 3D coordinates and is unaffected
by the pLDDT precision issue discussed in Section~\ref{sec:disc_precision}.
